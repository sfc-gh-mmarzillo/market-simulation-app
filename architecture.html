Market strategy simulation on Snowflake

Cut price to squeeze a competitor → it pivots capacity to other markets → we adapt. Illustrated for a battery manufacturer (demo company: Voltara Energy; synthetic data).

The business question

“If we cut Auto aftermarket prices to squeeze a low-cost competitor, will it pull out — and if it does, which of our other markets does its capacity flow into, how should we adapt, and does the whole move pay back?” Six application markets: Auto aftermarket · Auto OEM · Commercial / heavy-duty · Marine & powersports · Energy storage / industrial · Other. Competitors: Volt-X Imports (low cost), Apex Power (premium), rest of market.

The agent designs the strategy and explains it. Code computes every number. The Cortex Agent turns a plain-English question into strategy settings, runs the simulations, checks history and policy, and writes the recommendation. A vectorized Monte Carlo engine on fitted models produces every figure, and every result is stored with its full configuration.

Architecture — everything runs inside Snowflake

Market history

  • MARKET_HISTORY: monthly units, prices, share, capacity and cost per market × player
  • COMPETITOR_MOVES: every past capacity pivot (from → to)
  • SQUEEZE_EPISODES: what competitors did after our past cuts
  • Contracts & policy docs, incl. competition-law guardrails
2 · Models (23)

Snowpark Python, fitted once

  • 6 market demand (elasticity ± SE, growth, seasonality)
  • 6 share response (price and competitor capacity)
  • 3 competitor cost / price-follow
  • 3 pivot hazard (P(move capacity out) vs margin squeeze)
  • 3 destination choice (where the capacity goes)
  • Minor competitor price cuts · LME lead price
3 · Strategy engine

Python stored procedures

  • RUN_STRATEGY: monthly multi-phase Monte Carlo (cut → pivots → adaptation rule)
  • RUN_STRATEGY_SWEEP: cut depth × duration grid
  • Same random draws for strategy vs no-strategy baseline
  • About 10 s per strategy (5,000 paths × 24–36 months)

STRATEGY_RESULTS

  • Cumulative margin path, payback, P(loss)
  • Capacity flow matrix between markets
  • Results by market; competitor capacity over time
  • Full config and model-parameter snapshot per run

What happens when you ask the agent

  1. You ask in the app, e.g. “Cut aftermarket 12% for 6 months to squeeze Volt-X — where does it go and how should we adapt?” The app calls the agent through its own Snowflake session.
  2. The agent plans and turns the question into strategy settings: target market and competitor, cut depth and duration, adaptation rule (restore after N months / restore once the competitor exits / hold / follow it into its new market) and horizon.
  3. The engine simulates 5,000 monthly paths per option. Our price follows the strategy, competitors partly match it, their margin is squeezed, and in each path they may start pivoting capacity to another market. Shares, demand and margin are recomputed every month, and the same paths are rerun with no strategy as the baseline.
  4. The agent grounds the result: past squeeze episodes (what Volt-X actually did), the market outlook for the target and destination markets, and policy search, which flags Legal review for cuts deeper than 15% or longer than 6 months.
  5. The agent answers using only tool numbers: payback, P(loss), where capacity flows, market-by-market impact, and a recommendation framed as a proposal for approval.
  6. The app renders every scenario the agent ran on the Scenarios tab: its configuration, a flow chart, cumulative margin, volume shift by market, and competitor capacity over time.

Phased roadmap

BUILT

Phase 1 · Models + Monte Carlo

Market, share, competitor cost, pivot and destination models; multi-phase strategy engine running in seconds inside Snowflake.
BUILT

Phase 2 · Agent-built scenarios

Cortex Agent creates, runs, sweeps, stress-tests and explains strategies, grounded in past episodes and policy, with flow and outcome visuals for every run.

Phase 3 · Autonomous & governed

Scheduled market watcher (competitor capacity, lead price, demand); decision memo with human approval and an audit log; our own capacity redeployment as a second lever.
